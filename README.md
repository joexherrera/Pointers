QA Business Analyst (ETL/ELT)
Recruiter Interview Quick Notes
Main Message

I’m a senior, hands-on data professional who can translate business requirements, investigate data-load issues, validate results, and coordinate fixes through testing and release.

30-Second Introduction

“I’m a senior healthcare data professional with more than 20 years of experience in data warehousing, claims quality, application support, and data analysis. I work hands-on with SQL, PostgreSQL, Python, Snowflake, and enterprise data warehouses. At Elevance, I’ve validated source data, supported data loads, investigated load errors, repaired data, and coordinated defects and releases. This role is a strong fit because it combines data QA, business analysis, and technical troubleshooting.”

Top Selling Points
1. Direct Data-Load Experience
Validated incoming source data.
Supported production data loads.
Investigated load errors.
Performed data repairs.
Coordinated defects, fixes, and releases.

Say:

“I understand the full data-issue lifecycle—from source validation and load investigation through correction, retesting, and release.”

2. Strong SQL and Data Skills
Advanced SQL: joins, filters, aggregations, reconciliation.
PostgreSQL, Snowflake, Teradata, SAS, Python, and Excel.
Data validation, root-cause analysis, and warehouse logic.

Say:

“I use SQL to prove where a data discrepancy occurred rather than relying on assumptions.”

3. Business and Technical Bridge
Translate business policies into database logic.
Clarify expected outcomes.
Communicate findings to business owners and developers.
Document requirements, decisions, and technical processes.

Say:

“I bridge business intent and database reality.”

4. Strong QA Mindset
Validate accuracy and completeness.
Identify error patterns and root causes.
Compare expected versus actual results.
Confirm fixes before release.
5. Agile and Release Support
JIRA, Bitbucket, IntelliJ, Agile SDLC, and Scrum.
Defect tracking, data fixes, configuration changes, and releases.
Project-risk and dependency monitoring.
Common Recruiter Questions
“What ETL/ELT experience do you have?”

“My experience centers on the data integration and load lifecycle. I’ve validated source data, supported loads, researched load failures, repaired data, and coordinated defects and releases. I’m comfortable reconciling source and target data and identifying where a transformation or load failed.”

“How strong is your SQL?”

“SQL is one of my primary tools. I use joins, filtering, grouping, aggregation, and exception analysis to validate data, investigate discrepancies, and troubleshoot business logic.”

“Do you have UAT experience?”

“I’ve prepared test data, validated expected results, coordinated defects and corrections, performed retesting, and supported releases with business and technical teams. That experience transfers directly to formal UAT coordination and approval tracking.”

“Do you have source-to-target mapping experience?”

“I’ve worked with source validation, data definitions, warehouse tables, business rules, and load troubleshooting. I document source fields, transformation rules, target fields, validation requirements, and reconciliation criteria.”

“How do you investigate a failed load?”
Confirm the affected file, client, records, and fields.
Validate file structure, formats, required values, and duplicates.
Compare source, staging, and target data using SQL.
Review mappings, transformation rules, and configuration.
Coordinate the correction.
Rerun, reconcile, retest, and document the resolution.
“Why are you interested?”

“This role combines the work I’m strongest in: understanding business requirements, investigating data directly, resolving production issues, coordinating testing, and communicating with both users and engineering teams.”

“Are you overqualified?”

“I bring extensive experience, but I’m intentionally pursuing a hands-on role. I enjoy investigating data issues and staying involved through correction and validation.”

Compensation

“The posted W-2 range works for me. Based on my experience and the direct fit, I would target the top of the range at $62.50 per hour. I’m also interested in the long-term conversion opportunity.”

Work Schedule

“I understand that 8:30 a.m.–5:00 p.m. Eastern is 6:30 a.m.–3:00 p.m. Mountain, and I can consistently support that schedule.”

Questions to Ask the Recruiter
What percentage of the role involves production load-error investigation versus requirements and UAT?
What ETL/ELT tools, databases, warehouse platforms, and source formats does the client use?
What data-load problems are creating the greatest need for this position?
How is success measured during the contract period?
What is the expected conversion timeline?
Helpful Phrases
“I’m senior, but still highly hands-on with the data.”
“I bridge business intent and database reality.”
“I use SQL to isolate and prove root cause.”
“I stay with an issue from discovery through correction and validation.”
“I communicate technical issues in clear business terms.”
Do Not Overstate
Describe your experience as data-load QA and integration support, not full ETL pipeline development unless asked about work you actually performed.
Say JSON is a transferable skill unless you have substantial direct experience.
Describe UAT as testing, validation, defect coordination, and release support unless you formally owned scheduling and sign-off.
