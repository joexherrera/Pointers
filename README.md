For the recruiter screen, I would frame it like this:

“I’m currently at approximately $142K base, so since this would be a lateral move at the same grade level, I would not be looking to move backward in compensation. I’m very interested in the role because it aligns with my Snowflake, SQL, healthcare analytics, and reporting experience, but I’d want to understand whether there is flexibility to maintain or improve my current base depending on the scope of the role.”

That is professional, clear, and not too aggressive.

If the recruiter asks for your expectations, you can say:

“Given my current compensation and the posted range, I’d be targeting at least my current base, with some consideration for an increase depending on the responsibilities, hybrid expectations, and total compensation package. I’m open to learning more about the role and understanding what flexibility exists.”

If they say, “This is lateral, so we may not be able to increase pay,” you can respond:

“I understand. I’m still interested in learning more about the opportunity. Since I’m already slightly above the posted MRP, I’d want to make sure the move makes sense from a total compensation, growth, and career-development standpoint.”

I would not lead with “I need more money” in the first recruiter conversation. But I also would not let them assume you’re comfortable taking less or moving without any compensation discussion.

A reasonable target, if they ask directly, would be:

“Ideally, I’d be looking for something modestly above my current base, depending on the full scope of the role.”

If you want a number, I’d personally anchor around $145K–$150K, but only if they press you. Since you’re already above MRP, asking for a huge increase may be difficult for a lateral move. A modest increase is more realistic.

Also compare the full package: AIP/QIP percentage, hybrid commute costs, growth potential, visibility, future promotion path, and whether this role gets you closer to the data-driven career path you want. A lateral move can still be worth it if it gives you stronger Snowflake, executive reporting, financial analytics, or leadership exposure — but it should not require you to take a pay cut.
